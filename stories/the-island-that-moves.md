# The Island That Moves

At the edge of a city, where the hills folded down toward the bay, there was a little island that appeared only when the tide was thinking about leaving.

It wasn’t much to look at: three round stones, a bent reed, and a candle stub someone had set upright in the sand. But every evening, three visitors came to see it.

The first visitor brought a map. “We should decide what this place is for,” she said.

The second brought a notebook. “I’ll write down what happens,” he said.

The third brought a small bell. “I’ll ring this if anyone gets lost,” she said.

They worked until the sky turned purple. They measured the stones, named the reed, and debated whether the candle was a lighthouse or just a candle. Then a wave slid over the sand and rearranged everything.

The map was ruined. The notes ran together. The bell rolled into the water.

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then the first visitor laughed. “Well, now we know the island moves.”

The second took out a dry page. “And we know it doesn’t care what we call it.”

The third visitor waded after the bell, found it caught between two rocks, and rang it once.

From somewhere across the water came another bell, answering.

After that, they stopped trying to pin the island down. They came back each evening with one useful thing: a fresh page, a new map, a dry match. And whenever the tide changed the shape of the shore, they let it.

Years later, people said the island had taught them how to build a bridge.

But the three visitors knew better. The island had only taught them to listen for the answer.
