# The Second Moon

The town kept a second moon in a jar.

Every evening, just before dark, Mara carried it to the hill and loosened the lid. The little moon rose only as high as her hand before settling above the rooftops, bright enough to guide late walkers home.

One night, the jar was empty.

Mara searched beneath the bed, behind the flour bins, and inside the old clock tower. At last she found the moon hiding in the pocket of a boy who had never been allowed to stay out after sunset.

“I wanted to see where it went,” he said.

Mara considered this. Then she handed him the jar.

“Tomorrow,” she said, “you can help me put it back.”

And that night, for the first time, the town had two moons: one in the sky, and one carried carefully home by a child.
