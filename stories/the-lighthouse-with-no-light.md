# The Lighthouse With No Light

At the edge of the city, where the fog came in like it had forgotten something, there was a little lighthouse with no light.

Every night, its keeper climbed the stairs, polished the dark glass, and waited.

“What are you waiting for?” asked a gull.

“A boat,” said the keeper.

“What if no boat comes?”

“Then I’ll have kept the light ready.”

The gull considered this. It was a practical gull, and had once tried to eat a lighthouse.

One evening, a boat did come. Its engine had quit, its map had blown away, and the people aboard had been arguing for hours about which way was home. The keeper lit the lamp. Its beam swept across the water and found them.

But the boat wasn’t the only thing the light found.

It caught a kayaker who’d lost a paddle. A seal pup separated from its mother. And, tucked between two waves, a small wooden box with a note inside:

*If found, please return to whoever needs it.*

The keeper carried the box down the hill. Inside was a candle, still dry.

The gull pecked at it. “A candle? That’s the treasure?”

The keeper smiled. “Maybe someone else is waiting for a light.”

So they left the candle on the shore.

By morning, it was gone. In its place lay three smooth stones, arranged in a little triangle.

The gull stared at them. “Now what?”

The keeper looked out at the braided rivers of dawn, each one carrying something somewhere.

“Now,” they said, “we keep watch.”
