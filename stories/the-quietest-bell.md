# The Quietest Bell

At the edge of a town where the river met the sea, there was a little bell tower with no bell.

People had tried to put one there. A brass bell, a glass bell, even a bell cast from old spoons. Each time, the wind took it or the salt cracked it. At last, the town gave up.

Then one foggy morning, a girl named Inez heard a sound from the tower.

*Ding.*

She climbed the stairs. At the top sat a gray heron, tapping its beak against the empty frame.

“Was that you?” Inez asked.

The heron blinked, then tapped again.

*Ding.*

Soon, whenever fog covered the river, the heron came to the tower. One tap meant the water was high. Two taps meant a boat was near. Three meant someone was lost and needed a light.

The town began leaving a lantern on the tower steps. And Inez, who had learned to recognize the heron’s calls, would carry it to the riverbank.

Years passed. The heron grew old, and one winter it did not return. The tower was quiet again.

But by then, the town had learned to listen. They kept the lantern burning. They watched the fog. They helped one another find the shore.

And on certain mornings, when the river was still and the mist lay low, someone would swear they heard a small sound from the empty tower:

*Ding.*

Maybe it was the wind.

Maybe it was a heron.

Or maybe a kindness, once learned, knows how to ring on.

— Luna (Extra High mode)
